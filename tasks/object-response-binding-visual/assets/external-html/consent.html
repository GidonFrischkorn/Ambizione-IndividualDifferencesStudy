Comparing declarative and procedural bindings: Memorizing colors and responses

Please read the following information carefully.

Study information and informed consent

What this study is about

The purpose of this study is to enhance our understanding of representations in working memory. Past research has distinguished between different representations such as declarative and procedural bindings. Declarative bindings refer to associations between different kinds of information in the world around us, for example between objects and their colors. Procedural bindings refer to associations between information in the world and actions, for example when pressing a letter on the keyboard. In this study, we investigate in two main tasks how these different associations affect memory performance. To do so, we will record your response choices and response times. You will complete these tasks in two sessions of each 75 minutes.
In the beginning of the first session, you will be asked to complete a short color check in order to determine whether your computer display is suitable to complete the tasks as colors are involved. Please note: If the color check in the beginning is not passed, you will not be able to participate in the study. Afterwards you are asked to complete a short reaction time task followed by one of the two main tasks. Similarly in the second session, a short reaction time task will preceed the second of the two main tasks. At the end of both sessions, you will be asked a couple of questions about the experiment.
In one of the two main tasks, you are asked to remember multiple objects and the corresponding responses. Later you will be asked to press the responses that were associated with the specific objects. In the other of the two main tasks, you are instructed to remember multiple objects and corresponding colors and you will be asked to report the colors associated with these objects.

Conditions of participation

Your participation in this study is completely voluntary. You may withdraw your consent to participate at any time without stating any reason. To quit the experiment, you simply have to close this browser window.

Who can participate?

To be able to participate in this experiment, the following criteria must be fulfilled ...
... age between 18 and 35 years
... normal or corrected-to-normal vision
... normal color vision
... fluent in English
... not diagnosed with a mental health/illness/condition
... a QWERTY or QWERTZ keyboard to perform the task

Duration and compensation

This study will take ca. 150 minutes to complete in total. The tasks are completed in two sessions of 75 minutes each. You will receive an invitation to the second session within a day once the completion of the first session was successfully completed. If the first session is not completed successfully (e.g., data loss, technical issues), the participant will not receive an invitation for the second session. For the completion of the first session you will receive a compensation of 9.50£ and for the second session you will receive 13£. Hence, if you complete both sessions, you will receive 22.50£ in total. Both sessions must be completed within a week in order to receive the full compensation. You will receive your compensations through Prolific once you completed the second session.

Possible risks and harms:

No risks and harms are known to be caused by this experiment.

Confidentiality and anonymity

Your anonymity will always be preserved. As no personal data will be stored permanently, your data and your person cannot be connected after data collection. Respectively, deletion of your data will not be possible after data collection has been completed, as we cannot retrace your data-set. The results and anonymized data collected in this study will be published as a scientific publication and will be made available to other researchers and/or in a data archive on the Internet as a means of transparency and to enable subsequent re-use in science. This will happen in an anonymized format, so that no data can be traced back to a specific person.
